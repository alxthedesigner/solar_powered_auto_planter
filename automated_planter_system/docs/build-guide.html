Automated planter · build guide

Solar Rain Planter

A planter that waters itself from collected rain and runs on sunlight, with as few moving parts as possible. Rain fills a 55-gallon drum on a stand, and gravity carries it through one valve per zone to a 2 × 6 ft bed. There's no pump. A small solar panel keeps an ESP32 alive, waking every hour to check the soil. It runs with no outside power or network: a clock module keeps time and an SD card keeps every reading. When they're available, its readings reach the Commune suite through a Wi-Fi bridge, the SD card, or a settings page on your phone.

From Rainmaker 9000: gravity feed, water rail, per-zone valves, ¼″ tubing, ml per watering on a schedule From Plantwatery v12: solar + 18650 power through a 5 V boost, deep sleep, watering when the soil is dry
Raising the drum

How high, and on what

It doesn't have to be a metre off the ground. What matters is the height from the bottom of the drum to the drip line. That height (the head) is what pushes water out of the holes. Aim for 2 ft (60 cm) or more. More head waters faster but needs a taller, sturdier stand. Measure from the bed's soil surface, so a 1 ft raised bed needs the drum's bottom about 3 ft off the ground.

Build it to hold 500 lb or more. Set the legs on pavers, not bare soil.
  • Weight: 55 gal of water weighs about 460 lb, so a full drum is about 480 lb (220 kg). Put the stand on a level pad of compacted gravel or pavers, never soft soil. A tilt grows as the ground settles.
  • Strap it to a wall, fence post or the stand with a ratchet strap. A full drum is top-heavy.
  • Don't use a single wood pallet, a stand rated under 500 lb, or a tall stack of blocks without bracing.
  • Flow drops as the drum empties, because there's less water pushing. Watering is timed, so calibrate at half full (below), or add the optional flow sensor to measure each watering exactly.
What a zone is

Two zones, each watered its own way

A zone is a group of plants that share one valve, one drip line and one soil sensor. The 2 × 6 ft bed splits into two 2 × 3 ft zones. Each zone picks when to water on Commune's Planter page:

When the soil is dry · the defaultAt each watering hour, it waters only if the zone's soil is below its threshold. This uses the least water, as in Plantwatery.
On a scheduleIt waters the set amount at every watering hour, every N days (1 = daily, 7 = weekly), whatever the soil says, as in Rainmaker 9000. For hourly watering, set the watering hours to a range like 6-20.

What it does every hour

One wake cycle

  1. Wakes from deep sleep and, if the last start wasn't a planned wake, closes every valve first. Sets its clock from the clock module, and uses the SD card's settings file if it was edited. If the setup button woke it, it opens the phone page first.
  2. Powers the sensors for a moment and reads soil moisture per zone, soil and air temperature, battery, solar and the drum's float.
  3. At a watering hour, it waters each zone by that zone's mode, unless the drum is low, the battery is below its minimum or the zone has had its daily limit.
  4. Logs the reading and anything it did to the SD card. Every third reading, and straight after watering, it also posts to the bridge, whose reply brings new settings from Commune.
  5. Holds the valve pins low and sleeps until the next reading or watering hour. If Wi-Fi is down it keeps up to 48 readings and keeps watering on its last settings.
Low pressure matters

Gravity rules

  • No pressure-compensating drippers. They need about 1 bar, and the drum gives 0.06–0.15 bar. Use punched holes or open-ended drip tube.
  • Outlet near the bottom, not on it. Fit the bulkhead 2–3 in above the drum's floor so sediment stays behind.
  • A hand shutoff at the outlet lets you clean the filter or change a valve without draining the drum.
  • Optional pump add-on: if you later want the drum on the ground, set PIN_PUMP to 33. The firmware will then refill a small raised header tank from the drum (header full float on GPIO 18). It's off by default to keep moving parts to a minimum.

Build order

Building it

  1. Pad and stand. Level and compact a gravel pad, then build the block or timber stand on it. Set the empty drum on top and strap it.
  2. Plumbing. Fit the bulkhead, shutoff, filter, water rail, both valves and the drip lines. Then the gutter, first flush, inlet screen and overflow. Fill the drum and check every joint. Test each valve with 5 V from a supply, swapping its wires to close it.
  3. Power on the bench. Panel → TP4056 → cells → boost. Check for 5 V, then wire it to the ESP32 VIN and the DRV8833 VM.
  4. Driver and sensors. Follow the pin table, and bring every outdoor wire to a screw terminal.
  5. Firmware. Format the microSD card as FAT32 and put it in. Copy config_example.h to planter_config.h and fill in Wi-Fi, the bridge address and key, and a setup password. Then run pio run -t upload and watch pio device monitor. Press the setup button once to set the clock from your phone.
  6. Bridge and Commune. On the Raspberry Pi, run python3 -m commune_link.bridge --key …. In Commune's Planter page, enter the Pi's address and key, then calibrate and check each zone's settings. No Pi? Use the SD card and the phone page.
Ten minutes, once

Calibrate

  • Soil sensors. Note the millivolts the monitor prints with the sensor in air (MOISTURE_DRY_MV) and in water up to its line (MOISTURE_WET_MV).
  • Flow per zone. With the drum about half full, catch one zone's water for 60 s. Set MS_PER_ML to 60000 ÷ ml caught. Each watering is then about right on average: a little more when the drum is full, a little less when it's low.
  • Valve travel. Time a valve from shut to open and set VALVE_TRAVEL_MS about 1 s longer.
Fail safe

Safety

  • After any unplanned reset the board closes every valve first, and the valve pins are held low while it sleeps.
  • A valve never stays open longer than MAX_OPEN_MS, each zone has a daily limit, and nothing waters when the drum's float is low or the battery is below its minimum.
  • The stand carries about 480 lb: level pad, strapped drum, no soft ground.
  • Keep the box out of direct rain and the cells out of hot sun: lithium cells shouldn't charge below 0 °C or above 45 °C.
Growing the planter

Adding zones and drums

The firmware handles up to 8 zones and 4 drums. Each zone has its own valve, drip line and soil sensor, and is fed by one drum. When a drum runs low, only the zones it feeds stop watering. After two zones the ESP32 has no free pins, so a bigger planter adds two small boards to the clock module's I²C wires (SDA 21, SCL 22, 3.3 V). The boost still copes, because only one valve moves at a time.

  1. Wire the boards. Connect SDA, SCL, 3.3 V and GND on the expander and converter to the clock module's. Leave the address pins on GND for the first of each.
  2. Edit planter_config.h. Raise ZONE_COUNT and TANK_COUNT, and add one entry per zone to each list: soil sensor pin, both valve pins, MS_PER_ML, dry and wet millivolts, and ZONE_TANK (which drum feeds it). The end of config_example.h has a worked 4-zone, 2-drum example.
  3. Power the sensors. With more than 4 soil sensors, switch their power through a small P-MOSFET from pin 4: a single pin can't supply them all.
  4. Upload and calibrate the new zones as above. Then open Commune's Planter page: it shows every zone and drum the planter reports. Name them there and set each zone's watering.
